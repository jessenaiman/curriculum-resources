## Try
- Title: Flip the Addends
- Description: Build two addition sentences with the same counters in different order. Show that the total doesn't change when you swap the groups.
- Tip: Use two colours of counters on a part-part-whole mat. Let children physically turn the mat around to see the flip.

## Practice
- Title: Commutative Match Cards
- Description: Students cut and match expression cards that are equal because the addends are flipped (e.g., 3 + 4 ↔ 4 + 3).

## Check
- Title: Same Total? Thumbs Check
- Description: Say an equation aloud, then say it with the addends swapped. Students show thumbs up if the total stays the same, thumbs down if they think it changes.
- Tip: Use 5–8 quick examples. Include one subtraction pair to see if children notice the property doesn't always work.

## Planning note
Formal terminology such as "commutative" and "associative" should remain secondary at Grade 1 — focus on the observable pattern (swapping doesn't change the total) rather than naming the property. Ontario B2.1 frames this through the addition–subtraction relationship and fact families; keep strategies concrete and avoid abstract proof language. The Math Learning Center's Number Frames app supports visual demonstration but does not provide a single ready-made lesson sequence for this exact expectation.
