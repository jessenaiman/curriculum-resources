# Lesson Plans

These lesson plans are intended to demonstrate how to put
together a structured 30 minute lesson using the songs and
activities from the Let’s Play Music website. Each week we will
take a different theme and aim to develop a broad base of
musical skills in singing, listening, percussion, pitch, rhythm and
understanding.
Ideally the songs should be sung together during the class with
some form of accompaniment, piano or guitar would be great
but kids are still perfectly happy just singing along together with
the percussion instruments instead.
If you work through the material for each lesson plan as listed, it
should last around 30 mins, but it is always important to be
ﬂexible. Sometimes one activity might be particularly successful
and you’ll want to do it for longer, or sometimes things don’t go
quite as planned, so it’s always important to have a few old
favourites ‘up your sleeve’. Circle games and Circle time
songs are always popular, and you can just slot them in if you
have a few spare minutes to ﬁll. Choose a quieter activity if they
need to settle or something lively if they need to work off the
wiggles!
The Hello Song which is at the beginning of every lesson can
also be used as a Goodbye Song, this works particularly well in a
music class where the children just come together for a weekly
session. It’s a great way to get started and bring the class to a
close and helps little ones to understand the routine. If the music
lesson is just a part of the normal school day, you may prefer to
leave it out.
In the interest of clarity and ease of use, I have tried to avoid
making these plans too wordy, and so only give in-depth
directions for the more unusual or original activities. However, all
the material can be found for reference on the Let’s Play Music
website. Search for the titles in bold using the search box on the
right hand side of the homepage, to check for more details.
Colours
Focus - A lesson that teaches and practices colour recognition,
encouraging children to appreciate the wonderful colours of
everything around them. with a variety of musical fun activities.
• Learning solfege with chime bars or resonator bells
• Playing a variety of percussion instruments, recognising the
steady beat and the rhythm.
• Listening skills, remembering patterns and echoing rhythms
• Singing together with actions - speech development and solo
responses.
• Social skills; working in a group, following instructions and
building conﬁdence
• Imaginative movement to music.
Props needed:
• Resonator bells or a xylophone
• A variety of percussion instruments
• The printable sheet music and resources below.
• A cardboard staircase prop, to use with the little coloured
chime bar people.
• A selection of coloured scarves or ribbons and streamers to
use in musical movement.
This lesson is suitable for children from 2 - 6 years and should
take around 35 mins, but many of the activities can be used again
separately whenever you need a short engaging, musical activity
at any other time. These activities are also great for inspiring and
exploring colour before painting and craft activities.

2. The Do - Re - Mi Staircase - This game teaches children
the degrees of the solfege scale .
It teaches the correct order, the solfege colours and how they
sound.
Using a staircase to illustrate each step or note is an engaging
and visual way of teaching the spacial relationship between
notes and intervals in a way that children ﬁnd easy to
understand. This is such valuable preparation for learning to
play an instrument later on. I have found this game to be so
popular and useful as a teaching tool that it is really worth
including as a regular part of any preschool music lesson.
See Introducing Chime Bars for more information about
teaching chime bars)
Y ou can make your stairs by using our sheet below as a
template and mounting it on strong card.  My Do Re Mi steps
are cut out of wood, which you could do if you are handy
with a saw, or know someone who is. On the reverse side of
the staircase you need to make little pockets out of duck tape
behind each step so that you can slot the characters into
their correct position. Alternatively there is a Bell Step
Ladder available on Amazon
which would be more durable. Y ou could attach our little Do
Re Mi characters to wooden bricks which would work very
well and probably stand up to more use.
Once the children know which colour goes with which chime
bar there are loads of different games you can play:-
First make sure the children are familiar with the Do Re Mi
scale. Depending on the age of the children, take time to
introduce each character making sure the children are able to
recognise and name each colour and note. This can be spread
over several lessons.
Keep the chime bars hidden in a box or bag and use a
puppet to ask the children to help put the chime bars in the
correct order. Whilst you do this, place the little people onto
their corresponding stair on the staircase as the kids identify
each degree of the scale..
Let the children take turns to give the puppet clues about
which chime bar comes next;
This is perfect if you have a group of children with mixed
abilities, as the puppet can either help or be helped by the
child depending on their age and ability.
Start with low Do and work your way up the steps
until every note is in place.
Finally, sing the colour clues together like a little song using the
sheet below.
Another very popular game is to hide the chime bars or little
people around the room and let the children hunt for them,
identifying them and putting them in the correct order of the
scale as they go.
3. I Can Sing A Rainbow- Using the sheet music below, play
and sing together with piano  and percussion
instruments.
1st time identify and accentuate the steady beat.
2nd time, identify and accentuate the rhythm (the same as
the words)
3rd time, choose just the wooden instruments to play.
4th time, choose just the bells and triangles.
Last time, play and sing loudly all together.
4. If time allows you could read a short story book about

‘What makes A Rainbow?’

5. Magic Feet Follow The Beat! By now the children will be
ready for some movement.Use the sheet below to play this
game, it’s a brilliant way to have some fun with some lively
movement, while learning and practising the concept of note
values.
Use a tambourine to tap the beat, and once they have
‘rubbed the magic on their feet’, they are really in the mood
to stamp around with giant steps like Daddy whole note
or march like Big Brother 1/4 note
or run along with little fast steps like The Little 1/8 Note
Twins . When they’ve got the hang of each different
note and the sound and action it makes, you can experiment
with making them guess which one you are playing and try to
catch them out, or each child can have a go at tapping the
correct beat when you call out the note.
6. What’s My Colour? Song and Guessing Game
This song is brilliant for encouraging them to really listen to
the clues as well as building musical conﬁdence through call
and response.
My colour’s cool and pure,
Like sky or water ,
Guess what you think it is?
“I think it’s blue!”
*
My colour’s clean and fresh,
Like a cucumber,
Guess what you think it is?
“I think it’s green!”
*
My colour’s warm and hot,
Just like a chilli,
Guess what you think it is?
“I think it’s red!”
*
My colour’s like the sun,
Or a banana!
Guess what you think it is?
“I think it’s yellow”
*
My colour’s dark as ink,
Just like a spider!
Guess what you think it is?
“I think it’s black”
This song is a great one for circle time, as it really engages the
children and they are always so keen to show you how good
they are at listening to the clues and naming the colours.
For children of 2-3 years: you can use a variety of different
coloured props. This depends on what you have handy or
whether you are exploring a particular theme, but show the
correctly coloured item as you sing each verse, and they will
soon get the idea.
For children of 3-4 years: try hiding small coloured toys or bricks
in a bag. Choose one to hold in your hand – but don’t show it –
ask them to guess the colour by listening to the clues.
For children of 4-6 years: pass the bag around the circle, each
child takes a turn to pick an item and hide it in their hand. They
then sing the clues and everyone else guesses the colour.
Encourage them to experiment with different clues – there is
plenty of scope for some imaginative improvisation!
Y ou can develop this game further by making it into a treasure
hunt. Hide the objects around the room and ask the children to
search for each coloured item in turn. As you sing each verse
they will spot the different coloured items on their way, but they
must not touch them until that colour is requested. This also
serves as a memory game because they must try to remember
where each different coloured item can be found.
7. Free Musical Movement with brightly coloured, scarves,
ribbons or streamers. Put on some uplifting classical music
and invite the children to dance and twirl around the room
with their scarves and enjoy the colourful display.
