Welcome and Hello Song Recommendations

by Lindsey KrabbenhoftNovember 4, 2024November 4, 2024
Nov
4
2024

A welcome or hello song is the first song you sing at storytime. It is your first impression, your introduction, your tool for setting the tone of your program. Repeated every week, it creates a comforting routine for families. By starting with a song families have a chance to settle in and get sorted before you jump into the first story. The more I think about it, the more important it is to pick a welcome song that pulls its weight.

So let’s share!

I recently attended an Early Childhood Investigations webinar and learned a new one that I am excited to try out. It’s a new-to-me tune, easy to adapt with different verses, and I just love the message behind it. It’s called We’ve Been Waiting For You by Tom Hunter, Bev Bos, and Michael Leeman.

My old standby that I come back to again and again is Well Hello Everybody, Can You Touch Your Nose? By changing the action you can use it with any age – touch your toes with babies, clap your hands with toddlers, jump up high with preschoolers, or beep your belly with any age. You can also sing as many verses as you need. Still have families coming through the door? Just extend this song. End with “Well hello everybody, can you sit back down?” to transition into your first story.

What are your favourite welcome or hello songs? Feel free to share the lyrics or video links in the comments!

  4. Larissa Clotildes says:
    November 4, 2024

    I like “Hi, Hello, and How Are You?” to the tune of “London Bridge is Falling Down.” I think I got the first verse from jbrary (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aj4d6MLhJcE) and for a while I just used that. But then I wrote a second verse so I could tie in some social-emotional learning and pair it with some flannel faces.

    Are you happy?
    Are you sad?
    Are you scared?
    Are you mad?
    Are you happy, sad, scared, mad?
    How are you today?

    Then we talk about times we’ve felt those different feelings, make faces, and I encourage the kids to tell their grown-up how they feel right now (and the grown-up to tell their kiddos how they’re doing, too). Depending on the crowd, I might talk a bit about how we might feel any of these feelings during storytime: happy if we’re having run, sad when something ends, scared if its our first time, or mad if something doesn’t go the way we want it to. I started doing this during virtual storytimes in Covid, and I got some grateful feedback from parents who said that their kids felt really seen when we acknowledged that they might be feeling scared or angry because of pandemic conditions. Now I use it regularly.

    Then I close with a matching goodbye song, “Bye, Goodbye, and See you Soon”

    Bye, goodbye, and see you soon,
    See you soon,
    See you soon,
    Bye, goodbye, and see you soon,
    Have a lovely day!
