# SONG 1: I Went Down into the Barnyard

## LESSON PLANNING PROMPT

**Song:** I Went Down into the Barnyard
**Album:** Reaching for the Stars
**YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HIPiJHj6wKw
**Age Group:** Toddlers and Preschoolers (2-5 years)
**Staff Lead:** Miss Puddles
**Student Character:** Maisy

### Learning Objectives
- **Musical:** Steady beat through animal sound imitations; vocal exploration across pitch ranges (high chick peep, low cow moo); call-and-response form
- **Cognitive:** Animal identification and classification; vocabulary building (barnyard animals); sequencing (each verse introduces a new animal)
- **Physical:** Gross motor animal imitation movements; finger pointing; standing/sitting transitions
- **Social-Emotional:** Group participation; anticipation and surprise with each new animal; shared joy in animal sounds

### Materials
- Animal picture cards (cow, pig, chicken, horse, sheep, duck)
- Optional: rhythm sticks or hand drums for steady beat
- Optional: farm animal puppets

### Before (Introduction — 3 minutes)
Miss Puddles asks: "Has anyone been to a farm? What animals live on a farm?" Show animal picture cards. Count how many animals will visit the barnyard today. Establish the call-and-response pattern: teacher sings the animal sound, children echo.

### During (Core Activity — 5-7 minutes)
1. Sing the first verse together, emphasizing the animal sound
2. For each new verse, pause before the animal sound — let children guess what comes next
3. Add movements: cow (arms as horns), pig (wiggly nose), chicken (flapping elbows), horse (galloping in place)
4. On the repeat, invite children to sing the animal sound louder or softer (dynamics exploration)
5. Speed up slightly each verse if children are engaged (tempo variation)

### After (Extension — 3 minutes)
- Which animal was your favourite? Can you make that sound?
- Draw a barnyard — what animals would you put in your barnyard?
- Movement game: when I say an animal name, move like that animal

### Assessment Indicators
- Child can imitate at least 3 animal sounds with appropriate pitch
- Child can anticipate the next animal in the sequence
- Child participates in call-and-response without prompting

### Musical Focus
- **Beat:** Steady beat maintained through animal sounds and movement
- **Form:** Verse-repeating with new animal each verse; call-and-response
- **Dynamics:** Loud/soft animal sounds; crescendo through the song
- **Timbre:** Vocal imitation of different animal sounds; pitch variation

### Differentiation
- Younger toddlers: focus on 2-3 animals, use hand-over-hand guidance for movements
- Older preschoolers: let them choose the next animal, add counting ("how many animals?")
- Sensory-sensitive children: offer visual cards instead of loud animal sounds

## WORKSHEET PROMPTS

### Worksheet 1: Animal Sound Matching (Cognitive/Language)
**Purpose:** Match each animal picture to its sound
**Activity Type:** Matching/drawing lines
**Content:** Two columns — left column has pictures of barnyard animals (cow, pig, chicken, horse, sheep, duck), right column has the animal sounds written as words (moo, oink, cluck, neigh, baa, quack). Child draws a line connecting each animal to its sound.
**Differentiation:** Younger children match 3 animals; older children match all 6 and write the sound word.

### Worksheet 2: Barnyard Sequencing (Cognitive)
**Purpose:** Put the animals in the order they appear in the song
**Activity Type:** Cut and paste / ordering
**Content:** Six animal pictures in a jumbled row. Child cuts (or circles) the animals and places them in the correct order from the song: cow, pig, chicken, horse, sheep, duck.
**Differentiation:** Younger children order 3 animals with visual cues; older children order all 6 independently.

### Worksheet 3: My Barnyard Drawing (Creative/Art)
**Purpose:** Draw your own barnyard with the animals from the song
**Activity Type:** Drawing/free expression
**Content:** A large empty barn shape with the prompt "Draw the animals in the barnyard." Below the barn, a line for the child's name and the sentence: "My barnyard has ___ animals."
**Differentiation:** Younger children use stamps or stickers; older children draw independently and label animals.

### Worksheet 4: Animal Movement Cards (Physical/Gross Motor)
**Purpose:** Create movement cards for each animal
**Activity Type:** Cut and fold / matching
**Content:** Six cards with animal pictures on front. Child (with help) folds the card to reveal the movement instruction on back: cow = stomp slowly, pig = wiggle nose, chicken = flap arms, horse = gallop, sheep = bounce gently, duck = waddle.
**Differentiation:** Younger children act out with teacher guidance; older children read the instructions themselves.

### Worksheet 5: Counting Barnyard Animals (Math/Numeracy)
**Purpose:** Count the animals and write the number
**Activity Type:** Counting/writing
**Content:** Six boxes, each containing a group of animals (1 cow, 2 pigs, 3 chickens, 4 horses, 5 sheep, 6 ducks). Child counts each group and writes the number in the box.
**Differentiation:** Younger children count with teacher; older children count and write independently.

# SONG 2: Dig a Little Hole

## LESSON PLANNING PROMPT

**Song:** Dig a Little Hole
**Album:** Spring is Coming
**YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZZ1CpsJw4qg
**Age Group:** Toddlers and Preschoolers (2-5 years)
**Staff Lead:** Mr Sam
**Student Character:** Whiskers

### Learning Objectives
- **Musical:** Repetitive melodic pattern; steady beat through digging motions; dynamic contrast (soft digging, loud planting)
- **Cognitive:** Seed planting process; cause and effect (seed → plant); vocabulary (hole, seed, soil, water, sunshine)
- **Physical:** Fine motor digging motions; bending and reaching; miming planting actions
- **Social-Emotional:** Patience and waiting for growth; nurturing and care for living things

### Materials
- Real seeds and small pots with soil (optional hands-on extension)
- Picture cards showing planting stages: hole → seed → cover → water → sunshine → plant
- Green ribbon or streamer for "growing" movement

### Before (Introduction — 3 minutes)
Mr Sam asks: "What do you think a seed needs to grow?" Show a real seed. "Today we're going to dig a little hole and plant a seed — but first, let's sing about it!"

### During (Core Activity — 5-7 minutes)
1. Sing the song while miming the actions: dig, place seed, cover, water
2. Repeat with different dynamics: whisper the first verse (tiny seed), sing louder each verse as the plant grows
3. On the final verse, children stand tall and stretch arms up like a growing plant
4. Add a simple clap pattern on the beat during the chorus

### After (Extension — 3 minutes)
- If we actually planted a seed, what would we need to do every day?
- Act out the whole growing process from seed to flower
- Optional: plant real seeds in small pots

### Assessment Indicators
- Child can sequence the planting steps (dig → seed → cover → water → sun → grow)
- Child can demonstrate the growing movement (crouch to tall stretch)
- Child uses at least 2 planting vocabulary words

### Musical Focus
- **Beat:** Steady beat through digging motions
- **Form:** Verse-repeating with growing intensity
- **Dynamics:** Piano to forte as the plant grows
- **Tempo:** Gradual accelerando as excitement builds

### Differentiation
- Younger toddlers: focus on the digging and growing movements
- Older preschoolers: add counting (how many seeds?), discuss what plants need

# SONG 3: Over in the Meadow

## LESSON PLANNING PROMPT

**Song:** Over in the Meadow
**Album:** Zoom, Zoom Cuddle and Croon
**YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuVsgsEaZ94
**Age Group:** Toddlers and Preschoolers (2-5 years)
**Staff Lead:** Mr Sam
**Student Character:** Sam

### Learning Objectives
- **Musical:** Counting through song (1-10); repetitive melodic pattern with ascending structure; call-and-response; steady beat
- **Cognitive:** Number recognition (1-10); animal identification; animal habitat understanding; pattern recognition
- **Physical:** Finger counting; animal movements for each verse; hand gestures for numbers
- **Social-Emotional:** Turn-taking in counting; group participation; building confidence through repetition

### Materials
- Number cards 1-10
- Animal picture cards (turtle, fish, owl, rat, bee, crow, frog, lizard, duck, beaver)
- Optional: finger puppets for each animal

### Before (Introduction — 3 minutes)
Mr Sam counts to 10 with the children, holding up fingers. "Today we're going over to the meadow to meet some animal families — and each family has a different number of babies!"

### During (Core Activity — 10-12 minutes)
1. Sing verse 1 together (turtle one), holding up 1 finger
2. For each new verse, hold up the correct number of fingers
3. Children make the animal action for each verse (dig, swim, tu-whoo, gnaw, buzz, caw, jump, bask, quack, chew)
4. Speed up slightly as numbers increase — children love the challenge
5. On the final verse (ten beavers), everyone chews and claps together

### After (Extension — 3 minutes)
- Which animal family had the most babies? The fewest?
- Can you count to 10 on your fingers?
- Act out your favourite animal verse

### Assessment Indicators
- Child can count from 1 to at least 5 during the song
- Child can identify at least 4 animals from the song
- Child performs the correct animal action when the number is called

### Musical Focus
- **Beat:** Steady beat maintained through counting and animal actions
- **Form:** Verse-repeating with ascending number pattern (1-10)
- **Melody:** Repetitive melodic contour that rises with each number
- **Tempo:** Steady with optional accelerando for challenge

### Differentiation
- Younger toddlers: focus on numbers 1-5 with large movements
- Older preschoolers: count to 10, add subtraction ("if one frog hops away, how many left?")
- Sensory-sensitive: offer visual number cards instead of loud animal sounds

# SONG 4: Old MacDonald Had a Band

## LESSON PLANNING PROMPT

**Song:** Old MacDonald Had a Band
**Album:** Needs verification
**YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cadY2_t5gAU
**Age Group:** Preschool (3-5 years)
**Staff Lead:** Miss Puddles
**Student Character:** Maisy

### Learning Objectives
- **Musical:** Instrument identification; matching instruments to animal sounds; playing on the beat; ensemble playing; timbre exploration
- **Cognitive:** Animal-instrument association; sequencing (each verse adds an instrument); volume concepts (loud/soft)
- **Physical:** Fine motor instrument handling; rhythmic tapping/strumming; holding instruments correctly
- **Social-Emotional:** Playing together as a band; taking turns; listening to each other

### Materials
- Classroom instruments: tambourine, drum, triangle, shaker, rhythm sticks, xylophone
- Animal picture cards
- Optional: animal masks for each child

### Before (Introduction — 3 minutes)
Miss Puddles shows each instrument: "What sound does this make? What animal might make a sound like this?" Establish the association: drum = cow (moo/boom), triangle = chick (ting/peep), shaker = pig (shh/oink).

### During (Core Activity — 8-10 minutes)
1. Sing the first verse together; when the animal sound comes, play the matched instrument
2. Each new verse adds another instrument — children must listen for their cue
3. On "E-I-E-I-OOO," everyone plays their instrument together
4. Try playing some verses loud, some soft (dynamics)
5. Let individual children choose which animal/instrument pair they want

### After (Extension — 3 minutes)
- Which instrument was your favourite? Why?
- Can you make a band with three instruments? What three animals would they be?
- Free exploration of instruments

### Assessment Indicators
- Child can identify at least 2 instruments by sound
- Child can play an instrument on the beat during the song
- Child waits for their cue before playing

### Musical Focus
- **Beat:** Steady beat through instrument playing
- **Form:** Verse-repeating with call-and-response; cumulative (instruments added)
- **Timbre:** Exploration of different instrument sounds
- **Texture:** Building from solo to ensemble; layered sounds

### Differentiation
- Younger toddlers: focus on 2 instruments, free exploration
- Older preschoolers: take turns choosing instruments, try to play in rhythm

### Assessment Indicators

### Musical Focus
- **Beat:** Steady beat through casting/reeling motions

- **Dynamics:** Piano (waiting) to forte (fish bites)
- **Tempo:** Slow (waiting) to fast (reeling in)

### Differentiation
- Younger toddlers: focus on casting and reeling motions
- Older preschoolers: add counting (how many fish?), discuss fish types

### Learning Objectives
- **Musical:** Sustained melodic phrases; legato singing; gentle dynamics (piano); slow tempo for nighttime atmosphere; hand gesture choreography
- **Cognitive:** Owl behaviour; nighttime vocabulary; moon phases; Jane Yolen's book connection; nature observation
- **Physical:** Fine motor hand gestures (owl eyes, head turning); gentle swaying; quiet movement
- **Social-Emotional:** Calm and quiet emotions; bravery; hope; nighttime comfort; nature connection

### Materials
- Owl puppet or picture
- Moon picture or prop
- Optional: blue fabric for "night sky"
- Optional: copy of Jane Yolen's "Owl Moon" book

### Before (Introduction — 3 minutes)

### After (Extension — 3 minutes)

- Child maintains soft dynamics throughout the song
- Child can identify at least 2 things the song says you need (quiet, brave, hope, heat)

- **Beat:** Slow, sustained beat

- **Dynamics:** Consistently piano; no sudden loud passages
- **Tempo:** Slow and calm (nighttime atmosphere)

### Differentiation
- Younger toddlers: focus on owl eyes gesture and quiet singing
- Older preschoolers: discuss the book, explore the themes of bravery and hope

### Worksheet 1: Owl Nocturnal Match (Science)
