# Early Years Movement & Action Songs
**North American Folk & Educational Music Traditions (Infant – Kindergarten)**

A curated resource of songs that use body movement, gesture, and action to support early childhood development. Organized by developmental stage, then by activity type.

---

## INFANTS (0–18 months)

### Fingerplay Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open, Shut Them** | Traditional (also Hap Palmer) | Hands opening/shutting, clapping, patting knees, stretching arms overhead | Hand-eye coordination, fine motor control, listening skills, concept learning (open/closed, big/small) | Sit infant on lap; sing "Open, shut them, open, shut them, give a little clap" while guiding baby's hands through motions. Continue with "Open, shut them, sleep soundly" (tuck hands gently under chin), then "Open, shut them…give a little wriggle," etc. Adapt for infants: tap palms together, guide wrists gently. |
| **Pat-a-Cake** | Traditional | Clapping hands, patting laps, thumb-tapping (for baking motion) | Social engagement, turn-taking, rhythm awareness, memory, fine motor skill | Sing while clapping your palms together and patting the child's lap in rhythm. End by tickling ("toss it up and bake it"). Great for parent-child bonding. |
| **This Little Piggy** | Traditional | Toes/fingers — wiggling each digit, gentle pinching at toes | Foot/hand discrimination, tactile exploration, language-reasoning ("went to market") | Lift one toe or finger at a time, saying "This little piggy…" Pinch the last toe gently on "Wee!" before giggling. Do both feet and both hands across sessions. |
| **The Itsy Bitsy Spider** | Traditional (Hap Palmer, Kathy Reid-Naiman) | Index fingers tracing spider climb, hands tipping forward (rain falling), sun circle with arms | Fine motor sequencing, cause-and-effect understanding, pre-literacy (following a story) | Trace a spider climbing up a water spout using index fingers, then tilt hands down for rain, make a sun circle overhead. Extend upward reach as the spider climbs again. |

### Lullabies

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star** | Traditional (Hap Palmer adds new verse) | Arms raising slowly overhead (star rising), twinkling fingers, swaying body | Calming/self-regulation, spatial awareness, phonological sensitivity, vocabulary | Stand or sit with infant; raise arms slowly like a rising star, wiggle fingers while singing verses. Add Hap Palmer's second verse from Shakespeare. Rock gently side-to-side for soothing effect. |
| **Rock-a-bye Baby** | Traditional | Gentle rocking arm motion, cradling imaginary baby | Soothing/sleep association, rhythmic movement, trust and attachment | Hold infant close, rock rhythmically side-to-side or front-to-back. Keep movements slow and even. Excellent for transitions to rest. |
| **Brahms' Lullaby** | Traditional (Raffi also recorded) | Gentle rocking, soft stroking | Sleep preparation, sensory calm, emotional regulation | Cradle the child gently in your arms while singing or playing the recording. Use with dim lights and reduced noise to signal rest time. |

### Action / Movement Songs (Gentle)

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Smooth Road** | Kathy Reid-Naiman | Full-body sway (simulating boat ride on smooth road) | vestibular sensing, gross motor balance, calming rhythm | Hold infant securely and sway side-to-side like rolling on a smooth boat. Slow, rhythmic motion soothes and calms. |
| **Ladies Ride** | Kathy Reid-Naiman | Bouncing gently on knee, light bouncing rhythm | Rhythm sense, vestibular input, joy/movement association | Hold baby on your knee, bounce gently to the rhythm. Alternates sitting/bouncing which exercises core muscles. |

## TODDLERS (18 months – 3 years)

### Fingerplay Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fingers, Thumbs and Toes** | Kathy Reid-Naiman | Pointing to individual fingers, thumbs, toes | Body-part identification, counting 1-5, following directions | Sing while pointing to each finger/thumb/toe in sequence. Ask children to point independently. Extend by naming specific fingers ("Touch your pinky!"). |
| **Open Shut Them** | Kathy Reid-Naiman | Same as traditional — hands open, shut, clap, wink, stretch | Repetition practice, hand-eye coordination, listening comprehension | Lead group in familiar motions. For toddlers who can't follow all steps, model each action slowly and let them imitate. |
| **Colors and Actions** | Kathy Reid-Naiman | Specific body part + color actions (e.g., "touch something green") | Color recognition, following multi-step instructions, body-part vocabulary | Call out colors and ask children to touch objects matching that color. Alternate between body parts ("touch something blue!") and environmental objects. |
| **I'm a Little Teapot** | Traditional (Hap Palmer) | Arm as teapot spout, tip sideways, fill with water motion | Body awareness, imaginative play, pre-writing posture | Make fist with one hand on forearm of other arm. Tip sideways with arm as spout. Stretch tall like a boiling pot. Speed up for extra fun. |

### Lullabies

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sleep My Child** | Raffi | Gentle rocking, soft stroking | Sleep transition, self-regulation, emotional security | Sing during afternoon quiet time or evening wind-down. Hold children in lap or beside bed; maintain steady, gentle rocking motion throughout. |
| **You Are My Sunshine** | Traditional (Kathy Reid-Naiman arranges) | Smiling, hugging self, arms reaching outward | Attachment, emotional expression, positive self-concept | Sing with warm facial expression. Wrap arms around yourself for "you make me happy." Hug the child on the final line. Good for morning greetings or separation comfort. |

### Transition Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shake Your Sillies Out** | The Learning Station (traditional-style) | Shaking arms, legs, head, hips, whole body; freezing on cue | Body awareness, self-regulation, impulse control, gross motor | Sing and shake every body part mentioned ("shake your sillies out," freeze when music stops). Builds proprioceptive awareness and teaches body control. |

### Circle Time Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clap Everybody and Say Hello** | Kathy Reid-Naiman (album: *Sally Go Round the Sun*) | Clapping hands high, low, medium; calling names; waving | Greeting etiquette, social belonging, rhythm, name recognition | Gather in a circle. As song calls out names or general greetings, children clap corresponding to pitch (high/medium/low) and wave hello to friends. |

### Action Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes** | Traditional | Touching head, shoulders, knees, toes in sequence; eyes, ears, mouth, nose | Body-part identification, gross motor sequencing, speed/coordination challenge, following rapid directions | Start slow, touching each body part. Progressively speed up. Challenge toddlers to do it fast! Also works standing or seated. |
| **Wiggle Dance** | Danny Go (modern folk style) | Wiggling entire body, then isolated tiny wiggles (pinky, eyebrows, lips) | Gross and fine motor discrimination, body-part specificity, humor/expression | Follow along with dance moves: big wiggles → tiny wiggles. Ask toddlers to wiggle just their eyebrows or toes. Incorporate into brain break. |
| **Go Bananas** | The Learning Station | Mimic peeling banana, eating, monkey sounds, squishing, chewing | Imagination, gross motor variety, following directions, vocal expression | Perform each step: peel banana, take a bite, chew loudly, wipe mouth. Good for free-time transitions — keeps children in spot but active. |

### Action Songs (Body Parts)

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Touch Your Toes to Your Nose** | Hap Palmer | Bending to touch toes, bending to touch nose, alternating | Gross motor flexibility, body-part sequencing, balance | Follow along touching each body part as named in the song. Emphasize safe bending and balance. Hap's version includes playful verses that keep toddlers engaged. |
| **Where Is Elbow…Bendy Bendy Elbow** | Traditional (library storytime favorites) | Touching elbows, tummies, eyes (winking), hands (clapping), toes | Body-part vocabulary, bilateral coordination, response to verbal cues | Sing call-and-response: "Where is elbow?" children point then bend. Repeat for tummy (squish), eyes (wink), hands (clap). Great for library baby time. |

## PRESCHOOL / KINDERGARTEN (3–6 years)

### Circle Time Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The More We Get Together** | Traditional (Raffi version) | Holding hands in circle, walking around, waving, bringing people closer | Social cohesion, community-building, cooperation, friendship values | Form a circle holding hands. Walk around singing; on the last line, bring arms close. Switch leaders each chorus so different children lead. Warmup before group activities. |
| **Ring Around the Rosie** | Traditional (Hap Palmer — in *Early Childhood Classics* medley) | Walking in a circle, holding hands, dropping down on "ashes" | Group coordination, timing/dropping on cue, gross motor, historical curiosity | Walk holding hands in a circle; everyone drops to the floor on the final word. Discuss how this was likely a skipping game originally (not a plague reference — a common myth). |
| **The Mulberry Bush** ("Here We Go Round the Mulberry Bush") | Traditional (Hap Palmer medley) | Circling, washing clothes, drying, hanging, shaking (each verse adds an action) | Daily-routine vocabulary, cooperative storytelling, gross motor sequencing | Each verse introduces a new action performed in the circle (washing hands, folding arms, touching heads). Cumulative format reinforces memory and group participation. |
| **The Hokey Pokey** | Traditional (Hap Palmer medley) | Inserting limbs (right foot/left hand/right arm) in and out, turning around, shaking all over | Laterality (left/right discrimination), following sequential directions, full-body coordination | Children stand in a scattered position and perform each limb-action individually, then come together for "shake all over." Helps develop left-right awareness. |
| **London Bridge Is Falling Down** | Traditional (Ella Jenkins) | Interlocking arms above head as bridge, trying to break through, falling down | Cooperative play, timing, gross motor, social negotiation | Two children form the arch with linked arms; others pass underneath. On "falling down," the arch drops to catch someone. Rotates quickly. |

### Action Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **If You're Happy and You Know It** | Traditional (various artists including Kathy Reid-Naiman) | Clapping hands, stomping feet, shouting "Hooray," plus variations (nodding, jumping, etc.) | Emotional expression, following directions, cause-and-effect learning, group participation | After the standard verses, invite children to suggest new actions ("If you're happy and you know it, stomp your feet!"). Encourage creativity. |
| **Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes** | Traditional (Hap Palmer blues-rock version) | Same as toddler version, played faster with musical variation | Rapid body-part identification, gross motor speed, perseverance, challenge | Play Hap Palmer's bluesy rendition and encourage kids to match the tempo. Speed rounds are always a hit — builds confidence through mastery. |
| **I'm In the Mood** | Raffi | Marching, shaking, twisting, jumping, dancing freely | Joyful movement, self-expression, gross motor diversity, energy release | A full-body dance song where children march, shake, twist, jump to different rhythm cues. Perfect for brain breaks or indoor recess. |
| **Got a Love Bug** | Raffi | Pointing to oneself, scratching "itchy love bug" spots (nose, head, tummy, feet) | Body-part vocabulary, self-awareness, social-emotional warmth | Sing while pretending a "love bug" lands on different body parts. Children point and laugh at each location. Great for affection/emotion building. |
| **Five Little Ducks** | Raffi | Wiggling five fingers (ducks), arm flying away, waving goodbye, mother duck looking, ducks swimming back | Counting backward 5→1, number concepts, narrative comprehension, emotion (concern/relief) | Use fingers as ducks. Each verse removes one finger "swimming away." Mother duck waves as they go; arms spread wide looking; rejoice when all return. Reinforces subtraction concept. |

### Body-Part Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Banana Phone** | Raffi | Holding banana as phone, speaking into it, dialing fingers, mimicking talking | Pretend play, communication skills, fine motor (dialing), vocabulary (telephone) | Children hold bananas like phones, pretend to dial, and talk into the fruit. Ask questions: "Who are you calling?" Extends to imaginative storytelling. |
| **Apples and Bananas** | Raffi | Vocalizing vowel substitutions (apples = eppels, i pplls, etc.) | Phonemic awareness, vowel identification, speech articulation | Have children substitute different vowels as they sing. Encourage exaggerated mouth shapes for each vowel change. Supports early literacy. |
| **Shake a Toe** | Raffi | Shaking individual body parts from toe to head: toe, knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, neck, chin, head, eye, nose | Whole-body scanning, body-part vocabulary, gross motor isolation | Shake one body part per beat. Speed up near the end. Can be done standing or sitting. Raffi's groove makes it irresistible. |
| **Tony Chestnut (Toe Knee Chestnut)** | The Learning Station (folk tradition) | Touching toes, knees, chest, and head in sequence | Gross motor sequencing, body-part identification, lateralization, working memory | Sing and touch: toes → knees → chest → head. Song speeds up each verse. Challenge children to stay coordinated as tempo increases. |

### Transition Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Good-Bye Song** | Hap Palmer; Sharon Lois & Bram ("Skinnamarinky") | Waving hands, blowing kisses, hugging self, marching off | Routine transition (closure), greeting farewells, emotional security | Sing at the end of group time or class. Wave goodbye, blow kisses. Creates predictable closure ritual. Sharon Lois & Bram's "Skinnamarinky Dinky Doo I Love You" offers extra warmth at dismissal. |
| **Sit on the Mat and Clap** | Traditional (group circle-time staple) | Finding a spot in circle, sitting cross-legged, clapping on cue, quiet listening | Self-regulation, transitions to seated attention, listening skills, group belonging | Play the song; children find a spot, sit, and clap when prompted. Gradually teach quiet sitting after the clapping ends. Used for circle-time entry. |
| **A Root Chy Cha** | The Learning Station | Following dance-direction sequence (root-chy cha steps) | Following multi-step sequences, working memory, motor planning, listening | Sequence of choreographed dance steps taught gradually. Build up the full pattern over repetitions. Builds executive function through memory-based movement. |

### Lullabies

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **You Are My Sunshine** (calm version) | Traditional (Raffi includes in lullaby repertoire) | Gentle swaying, soft arm-hug motion | Self-soothing, emotional comfort, calm transition to rest | Sing quietly with slow tempo during afternoon wind-down. Children may hug themselves or a stuffed animal. |
| **Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star** (extended version) | Traditional (Hap Palmer's version with Shakespeare addition) | Stars twinkling fingers, slow arm-raising, gentle swaying | Language exposure (Shakespeare excerpt), peaceful imagery, calming routine | Sing full extended version with poetic second verse. Use twinkling fingers as "stars." Excellent for nap-time prep or bedtime. |
| **Softly and Gently** | Traditional (popularized by Pete Seeger — folk tradition) | Arms sweeping slowly, gentle rocking | Deep relaxation, slow motor control, breath awareness | Sing at very slow tempo. Children mimic large slow arm sweeps or rock gently. Ideal for deep-rest periods. |

### Fingerplay Songs

| Title | Artist | Body Parts / Movements | Educational Goal | Activity Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ten Little Fingers and Ten Little Hands** | Kathy Reid-Naiman | Pointing to each finger and hand, flexing joints, crossing arms | Body-part precision, laterality (left vs right hand), counting, visual tracking | Slowly count 1-5 on fingers of each hand. Cross arms over chest. Ask children to show right hand, left hand. Repeating weekly builds vocabulary fluency. |
| **Five Little Monkeys** | Traditional | Five fingers up, "jumping on the bed," bumping heads, phone-call gestures | Number counting (5 down to 0), safety awareness, sequencing, expressive language | Count down from five on fingers. Act out monkey bumps. Use thumb as "phone" to call doctor. Discussion of safety follows naturally. |
| **Bingo** | Traditional (Hap Palmer — extended with phonics verses) | Clapping letters of B-I-N-G-O, stepping, stamping | Phonemic awareness, letter recognition, spelling, memory | Instead of saying letters, clap/stomp them. Progressive elimination (last verse no claps) challenges working memory. Hap adds fun new verses with animal sounds. |

## CROSS-AGE RECOMMENDATIONS

Many of these songs work across all three age bands with simple adaptations:

| Song | Infant adaptation | Toddler adaptation | Preschool/Kindergarten adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head, Shoulders Knees & Toes | Touch adult's body parts | Touch own body parts | Speed rounds, chant without music |
| Open, Shut Them | Guided hand movements | Independent hand movements | Add new verses, add clapping patterns |
| Twinkle Twinkle Little Star | Gentle rock, dim light | Finger twinkle, slow arms | Recite Shakespeare verse, draw stars |
| Five Little Ducks | Finger puppets on hands | Finger-counting | Write equations (5-1=4), act out drama |
| The More We Get Together | Held on lap, swaying in circle | Hold hands, walk in circle | Choose leader, add welcome greetings |

## KEY ARTISTS AND THEIR CONTRIBUTIONS

| Artist | Specialty | Notable Movement/Action Contributions |
|---|---|---|
| **Kathy Reid-Naiman** | Canadian educational/fingerplay specialist | *Sally Go Round the Sun*, *Say What You See* (colors/actions), *Fingers, Thumbs & Toes*, *Clap Everybody and Say Hello*, extensive fingerplay repertoire |
| **Raffi** | Canadian folk children's icon, "Decommodification of Childhood" advocate | *Baby Beluga*, *The More We Get Together*, *Down By the Bay*, *Got a Love Bug*, *Five Little Ducks*, *Shake a Toe*, *Going On a Picnic*, gentle lullabies |
| **Hap Palmer** | U.S. early childhood classics pioneer | *Early Childhood Classics* album — expanded versions of 15 traditional songs with phonics/body/part extensions, instrumental tracks for sing-along |
| **Ella Jenkins** | U.S. "Godmother of Children's Music," Smithsonian artist | *Get Moving with Ella Jenkins* — *Stop and Go*, *Hop Skip Jump to My Lou*, *Teddy Bear*, *Pole Pole* (slow-slow — movement regulation) |
| **Sharon Lois & Bram** | Canadian children's entertainment trio (*The Elephant Show*) | *Skinnamarinky Dinky Doo I Love You*, *Waddalyatcha*, camp/action songs, elephant circus narratives |
| **Traditional Folk** | Unattributed, centuries-old North American/Brit Isles nursery rhymes | *Head Shoulders Knees & Toes*, *Open Shut Them*, *Itsy Bitsy Spider*, *Ring Around the Rosie*, *London Bridge*, *This Little Piggy*, *Pat-a-Cake*, *If You're Happy and You Know It* |

## DEVELOPMENTAL BENEFITS SUMMARY

| Skill Domain | Example Songs | Developmental Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross Motor** | Head Shoulders Knees & Toes, Shake Your Sillies Out, I'm In the Mood | Large muscle coordination, balance, spatial awareness, cardiovascular health |
| **Fine Motor** | Open Shut Them, This Little Piggy, Pat-a-Cake | Small muscle control, pincer grasp, hand dexterity, pre-writing skills |
| **Body Awareness** | Fingers Thumbs & Toes, Shake a Toe, Where Is Elbow | Proprioception, laterality (left/right), body schema development |
| **Language/Literacy** | Apples and Bananas, Bingo, Down by the Bay | Phonemic awareness, rhyme recognition, vocabulary, vowel sounds, letter identification |
| **Social-Emotional** | The More We Get Together, Skinnamarinky, Got a Love Bug | Friendship, cooperation, empathy, self-expression, belonging |
| **Self-Regulation** | Stop and Go, Twist and Shout songs, lullabies | Impulse control, body awareness, calming strategies, emotional management |
| **Cognitive/Math** | Five Little Ducks, Bingo | Counting, one-to-one correspondence, subtraction concepts, letter sequencing |
| **Following Directions** | If You're Happy and You Know It, A Root Chy Cha, Hokey Pokey | Working memory, sequential processing, listening skills, executive function |
