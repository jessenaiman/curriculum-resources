## Key Findings

### ✅ What IS Reliable
1. **Artist identities and biographies** — All nine artists are genuinely real, influential figures in North American early-years music.
2. **Raffi's catalog** — With 10/14 verified, Raffi's entries are the most reliable section. *Baby Beluga*, *Shake My Sillies Out*, *Everything Grows*, *One Light, One Sun*, *Apples and Bananas*, and others are accurately attributed.
3. **Traditional song recognition** — The document correctly identifies many traditional/public-domain songs (e.g., "The Cat Came Back," "London Bridge," "Do Your Ears Hang Low?").

### ⚠️ Systematic Issues
1. **Traditional songs presented as artist originals** — Numerous entries attribute century-old folk songs (many predating the artists by 50-200+ years) to specific artists without clarifying these are covers/adaptations. Examples: "Five Little Ducks," "This Little Light of Mine," "Ring Around the Rosy," "She'll Be Comin' Round the Mountain," "Frog Went A-Courtin'," "The Itsy Bitsy Spider."
2. **Fabricated album titles** — Several album names appear invented or conflated with real releases (e.g., *Seeger Recordings for Children*, *Playparty Songs*, *Children's Songs of Protest and Hope*, *Songs Children Love to Sing*).
3. **Weak sourcing** — Heavy reliance on Instagram posts, Reddit threads, fan wikis, and generic library CD listings rather than official artist websites, Discogs, or streaming platforms.
4. **Non-lyric "lyrics"** — Several entries list activity descriptions or lesson-plan language in the "Lyrics Snippet" field (e.g., "Each letter receives its proper sound," "Students create their own animal songs").

### ❌ Clear Hallucinations (5 entries)
1. **Pete Seeger — "One Grain of Sand"** — Entirely fabricated song/album combination.
2. **Ella Jenkins — "Caring for the Environment"** — An educational topic label, not a song title. Lyric field contains a description, not lyrics.
3. **Fred Penner — "We Compare"** — A curriculum activity, not a song title.
4. **Dan Zanes — "Little Broken Truck"** — Misplaced (in Dan Zanes section but credited to Elizabeth Mitchell on *Baby Banjo*), likely fabricated, lyrics read as activity instructions.
5. **Elizabeth Mitchell — "Ask the Students"** — A lesson plan title, not a song. Lyrics describe student creative writing activity.

### 🔧 Recommended Corrections
- Distinguish clearly between **original compositions** and **traditional songs** covered by these artists.
- Replace vague/non-existent album citations with verifiable Discogs/official-source data.
- Remove entries where the "lyrics" field contains activity descriptions instead of actual lyrics.
- The document needs approximately **40-50 entries removed or rewritten** to meet academic accuracy standards. Only about 15% of entries (primarily Raffi's) have high confidence ratings.
