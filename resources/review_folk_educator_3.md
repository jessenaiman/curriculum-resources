### Category 1: Counting & Numbers (8 songs)

**Developmental Verification:**
- **Ten Galloping Horses (2-5y):** ✅ Counting 1-10 is appropriate for this range. By age 3, most children can count to 5; by age 5, many reach 10. The galloping motion adds gross motor integration.
- **One Two Buckle My Shoe (2-5y):** ✅ Classic counting rhyme with opposites — excellent for phonological awareness and number concepts.
- **Five Little Peas (2-5y):** ✅ Counting 1-5 is perfect for this age. The "growing" gesture supports understanding of plant life cycles.
- **Five Little Pumpkins (2-5y):** ✅ Counting 1-5 with emotions — excellent for socio-emotional learning.
- **Five Little Daffodils (2-5y):** ✅ Counting 1-5 with spring theme — good seasonal connection.
- **Hallowe'en Counting Rhyme (2-5y):** ⚠️ "Gentle scares" may be too intense for children under 3; recommend for 3+ only.
- **One Button Two Buttons (6mo-3y):** ✅ Counting 1-2 is appropriate for infants. Fine motor focus is excellent.
- **Ten Little Angels (2-5y):** ✅ Counting 1-10 — "angel" vocabulary is neutral; the flying motions add engagement.

**Missing Actions:**
- Ten Galloping Horses: **Add:** stomping on each count; counting fingers while galloping
- One Two Buckle My Shoe: **Add:** shutting and opening hands for "shut/open"
- Five Little Peas: **Add:** popping fingers open like peas; counting each "pea"
- Five Little Pumpkins: **Add:** making pumpkin faces; pointing to gate
- Five Little Daffodils: **Add:** swaying in the wind; pointing to yellow
- Hallowe'en Counting Rhyme: **Add:** counting on fingers; gentle spooky gestures
- One Button Two Buttons: **Add:** touching buttons on clothing; counting
- Ten Little Angels: **Add:** showing fingers one at a time; gentle flying

**Category Assessment:** Strong counting progression from 1-2 (infants) to 1-10 (preschoolers). The physical integration of counting with movement is excellent for developing one-to-one correspondence.

### Category 2: Body Parts & Self-Awareness (9 songs)

**Developmental Verification:**
- **Tommy Thumb (6mo-3y):** ✅ Classic fingerplay — finger isolation develops around 9-12 months. Perfect timing.
- **Open Shut Them (6mo-3y):** ✅ Hand awareness + opposites — excellent for fine motor and cognitive development.
- **Clap Your Hands (6mo-3y):** ✅ Clapping emerges around 6 months — appropriate.
- **Palm Tickles / Face Tickles (6-18mo):** ✅ Sensory awareness + trust — essential for infant development.
- **Toe Tittly (1-5y):** ⚠️ Wide age range; the foot tickling aspect may not interest preschoolers over 3. Better for 1-3y.
- **Shake That Little Foot Dinah O (1-5y):** ✅ Foot/leg awareness — good for body exploration.
- **Everybody Knows I Love My Toes (1-4y):** ✅ Self-love + humor — excellent socio-emotional content.
- **Wiggle Your Fingers (6mo-3y):** ✅ Fine motor focus — appropriate.
- **Head and Thorax (3-7y):** ⚠️ **TOO ADVANCED** for 3-year-olds; "thorax" and "abdomen" are scientific vocabulary more suited to 5-7 years. Recommend narrowing to 5-7y.

**Missing Actions:**
- Tommy Thumb: **Add:** hiding thumbs behind back; counting to five
- Open Shut Them: **Add:** hiding hands behind back; showing ten fingers
- Clap Your Hands: **Add:** various clapping patterns; clap on different body parts
- Palm Tickles: **Add:** anticipatory giggles; reaching for more
- Toe Tittly: **Add:** wiggling toes; touching each toe
- Shake That Little Foot: **Add:** shaking while seated; shaking while standing
- Everybody Knows I Love My Toes: **Add:** touching and wiggling toes; counting
- Wiggle Your Fingers: **Add:** hiding and showing fingers; wiggling in air
- Head and Thorax: **Add:** touching head, chest, belly; learning insect body parts

**Category Assessment:** Good progression from infant sensory to preschool body awareness. One song flagged as too advanced for its age group.
