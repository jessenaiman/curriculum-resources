## About Kathy Reid-Naiman

Kathy Reid-Naiman is one of Canada's leading early childhood recording artists, with over 30 years of experience creating music for young children. She has produced 25+ CDs since 1994 through her self-run label, Merriweather Records Ltd. Her recordings have been included in Newborn Literacy Kits distributed by Early Years Centres and Library Associations across multiple Canadian provinces (Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta) to encourage parental involvement in early learning and literacy.

Kathy teaches an enriching music program in libraries and schools throughout Ontario, a program she has been developing since 1980 when her own children were young. Her work with the **Parent-Child Mother Goose Program** has been central to her approach, collaborating on multiple recordings from programs across Canada.

## 1. Teaching Philosophy

### Core Beliefs

- **Music as a gateway to language and literacy**: Kathy believes that introducing children to the wonders of language and music through song is foundational to early development.
- **High-quality, professional recordings**: A great recording should transition smoothly between songs with varied musical arrangements — not just simple, repetitive tracks. This "simple, but disciplined approach" is why she has been recognized as a leading early childhood artist.
- **Accessibility for all**: Her music is designed so that "adults secretly confess to listening to it just for themselves." The comfort of tradition and sweetness of nostalgia combined with novelty and innovation makes the music engaging for both children and caregivers.
- **Parent-child interaction**: Many of her recordings and programs are designed for parents and children to experience together, strengthening the caregiver-child bond through shared musical experiences.
- **Traditional and contemporary fusion**: Kathy draws from traditional folk songs, nursery rhymes, and parent-child program repertoire while also creating new compositions. She incorporates diverse musical styles including old-time, calypso, swing, klezmer, skiffle band, and gentle lullabies.

### Approach to Early Childhood Music

Kathy's approach is rooted in the understanding that music and movement are natural vehicles for early learning. She combines:

- **Singing games and dances** — for active participation and gross motor development
- **Tickles and knee bounces** — for sensory stimulation and caregiver-child bonding
- **Fingerplays** — for fine motor skills, language acquisition, and cognitive development
- **Lullabies** — for soothing, emotional regulation, and bedtime routines
- **Rhythm instruments** — for auditory discrimination, coordination, and self-expression

## 2. Recommended Daily Routines Using Songs

Kathy's music is organized to support different parts of the day, as reflected in her album structure:

### Morning / Active Times
- **"Say Hello to the Morning"** — Songs, singing games, tickles, and knee bounces for toddlers and preschoolers. Features diverse musical styles (old time, calypso, swing, klezmer, skiffle band) to capture the sense of fun in weekly music classes.
- **"Sally Go Round the Sun"** — A collection of songs and rhymes designed to use *through the day*. Collaborated with the Parent-Child Mother Goose Program.
- **"Here We Go Zodeo"** — Singing games and dances for children of all ages. Tracks are full dance length so they can be easily used in the classroom when a live musician is not available.
- **"I Love to Hear the Sounds"** — Songs selected for use with rhythm instruments: sticks, bells, shakers, sand blocks, drums, and rhythm band. Includes instrumental versions at the end for teachers to use with their own voices and adapt for different instruments.

### Quiet / Transition Times
- **"On My Way to Dreamland"** — 19 lullabies and songs for quiet times. Includes well-known songs like "Lavender's Blue" and classics like "Catch a Falling Star."
- **"Sally Go Round the Moon"** — Quiet songs and lullabies to soothe babies all through the night. The companion to "Sally Go Round the Sun."

### Seasonal Routines
Kathy created a complete **Seasons Series** that can be used to structure music throughout the year:

| Season | Album | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **Winter** | *Sing the Cold Winter Away* | Songs about snow, family gatherings, Chanukah, Christmas, Winter Solstice, New Year's Eve |
| **Spring** | *Spring is Coming* | 30 springtime lullabies, rhymes, fingerplays, dances, and songs |
| **Summer** | *Welcome Summer* | Songs about birds, bees, butterflies, campfires, and starry skies |
| **Autumn** | *When It's Autumn* | Autumn-themed songs and activities |

### Bedtime / Home Routines
- **"Baby's Boat"** — Songs, rhymes, tickles, and dances specifically for babies. The "cream of the crop" from Kathy's 20+ years of recordings.
- **"Zoom Zoom Cuddle and Croon"** — Best-loved songs, rhymes, and bounces from earlier recordings, created for the BC Library Association's Books for BC Babies early literacy program.

## 3. Activity Ideas for Different Age Groups

### Infants & Young Babies (Birth – 12 months)

**Recommended Albums**: *Baby's Boat*, *Zoom Zoom Cuddle and Croon*, *Timeless Tickles and Tunes*

| Activity Type | Description | Developmental Focus |
|---------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Lullabies** | Gentle singing while holding or rocking baby | Emotional bonding, auditory development, soothing |
| **Tickles** | Songs with gentle touch interactions | Sensory stimulation, caregiver-child connection |
| **Knee bounces** | Bouncing baby on caregiver's knee to rhythm | Vestibular development, rhythm awareness |
| **Knee joggles** | Gentle rocking movements with songs | Body awareness, trust building |

**Key principle**: At this age, the focus is on the caregiver-child interaction. The music provides a shared experience that strengthens attachment and introduces the sounds and rhythms of language.

---

### Toddlers (6 months – 3 years)

**Recommended Albums**: *Tickles and Tunes*, *More Tickles and Tunes*, *Say Hello to the Morning*

| Activity Type | Description | Developmental Focus |
|---------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Singing games** | Simple repetitive songs with actions | Language development, memory, participation |
| **Action songs** | Songs with hand/body movements | Gross motor skills, coordination |
| **Fingerplays** | Stories told with hands and fingers | Fine motor skills, dexterity, vocabulary |
| **Tickles & bounces** | Interactive songs with physical play | Sensory processing, social bonding |
| **Rhythm instruments** | Simple shakers, bells, sticks | Auditory discrimination, cause-and-effect |

**Activity Ideas**:
- Start each session with a **welcome song** (e.g., "Say Hello to the Morning")
- Use **transition songs** between activities (e.g., "Sally Go Round the Sun")
- Include **instrument play** with age-appropriate shakers and bells
- End with a **calming lullaby** or quiet song

## 4. How Songs Connect to Developmental Milestones

### Language & Literacy Development

Kathy's work has been specifically recognized for its contribution to **early literacy**:
- Her recordings are included in **Newborn Literacy Kits** distributed by Ontario Early Years, BC Books for Babies, and Edmonton Public Libraries
- These programs aim to "encourage parental involvement in early learning and literacy"
- Songs and rhymes develop phonological awareness, vocabulary, and narrative skills
- Repetition in songs supports memory and word recognition

### Fine Motor Skills (Fingerplays)

From the *Fingerplays DVD* description:
> "When a child watches the actions his mind is working furiously to process and understand the motions. By doing the actions he is exercising his small motor skills, increasing his dexterity and at the same time learning new words and ways to express himself. When a child and a parent learn a fingerplay together, it becomes another wonderful way to interact, share and learn."

**Key developmental benefits of fingerplays**:
- Small motor skill development
- Increased dexterity
- New vocabulary and expression
- Cognitive processing of motion and language simultaneously
- Shared learning experience between parent and child

### Gross Motor Development
- **Dancing and singing games** develop coordination, balance, and spatial awareness
- **Knee bounces and joggles** develop vestibular sense in infants
- **Marching and movement songs** build body awareness and rhythm

### Social-Emotional Development
- **Caregiver-child bonding** through shared musical experiences
- **Group singing and dancing** builds community and cooperation
- **Lullabies** support emotional regulation and self-soothing
- **Predictable routines** with songs create security and trust

### Cognitive Development
- **Pattern recognition** through musical structure
- **Memory development** through song repetition
- **Sequencing skills** through action songs and fingerplays
- **Cause-and-effect understanding** through instrument play

## 7. Tips for Using Kathy's Music in the Classroom

### Getting Started
1. **Start with one album** that matches your children's age group
2. **Learn the songs yourself** before introducing them to children
3. **Use the same songs regularly** — children thrive on repetition and predictability
4. **Observe what the children respond to** and build from there

### Building a Music Routine
1. **Opening song** — Start each session with a familiar welcome song
2. **Active songs** — Move to action songs and singing games
3. **Instrument play** — Include rhythm instrument time
4. **Quiet time** — Transition to calmer songs or lullabies
5. **Closing song** — End with a predictable farewell song

### Adapting for Your Setting
- **Small spaces**: Use fingerplays and seated songs instead of full dances
- **Large groups**: Use the full-length dance tracks from *Here We Go Zodeo*
- **Mixed ages**: Choose songs with simple actions that older children can add complexity to
- **Bilingual settings**: Use the English/French albums for language immersion
- **No instruments**: Many songs work beautifully without any instruments — just voices and bodies

### Connecting to Curriculum
- **Literacy**: Use songs to introduce new vocabulary, letter sounds, and story structure
- **Math**: Counting songs, pattern recognition in music, rhythm and measurement
- **Science**: Seasonal songs connect to nature studies and weather
- **Social studies**: Folk songs and cultural songs explore community and heritage
- **Physical education**: Dancing, marching, and movement songs support gross motor development
- **Social-emotional learning**: Lullabies and calming songs support emotional regulation
